Parimere: Google Play account and release guide
Official requirements checked on 2026-10-21.

October 22: the implementation-specific scope below describes the retained
Capacitor wrapper. New React Native development is in ../parimere-mobile.
Use that folder's README.txt and actual signed build when preparing its listing
and declarations; its first version does not include OCR, scanning or printing.

Parimere is being prepared as a web app and an Android phone app. This guide
explains how to create the publishing account and what must be finished before
a Play release. It does not certify a build, create an account, or publish an app.

Current release scope and open items

The Android release being prepared bundles the interface and calculation code
locally using Capacitor. Its intended scope includes offline comparisons, saved
comparisons, selected-photo OCR/barcodes, manual exchange rates, file exports
and sharing, and deletion of local app data. Household sharing, automatic rate
requests, ads, billing and subscriptions are excluded from this first native
build. The web app retains its existing web features. Optional Android ads
remain a future decision recorded in ANDROID_RELEASE.txt.

Treat that scope as the release specification. Verify it against the final
signed build and its network traffic before answering Play Console forms.
An Android project or a successful web test does not establish that Android
compilation, installation, camera/file access or store review has passed.

The following information and evidence are still needed:

[ ] Publisher's legal name or organization, and correct account type.
[ ] Public developer name, support email and privacy contact.
[ ] Permanent Android application ID, confirmed before the first upload.
[ ] Initial distribution countries and intended audience age groups.
[ ] Public HTTPS website and final privacy-policy URL.
[ ] Actual host/provider logging, retention and deletion information.
[ ] Android toolchain, successful release build and protected signing keys.
[ ] Tests on physical Android devices and the Play-delivered build.
[ ] Store screenshots, graphics, completed declarations and review access.
[ ] Verified developer account and any required closed-test evidence.

Any privacy page supplied with the project is a draft until the publishing
identity, contact details and deployed services are completed and reviewed.
Do not submit placeholder URLs or identify that draft as an approved policy.

1. Create the developer account

Use the Google account that should own Parimere long term. Keep its recovery
details current and enable two-step verification. Open:
https://play.google.com/console/signup

The account owner must be at least 18. Read and accept the Developer
Distribution Agreement and pay the US$25 one-time registration fee. Google
lists accepted credit/debit cards; availability varies by location and prepaid
cards are not accepted. Complete payment and identity steps yourself in the
Google interface. Never put identity documents or card details in the repo.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435

Choose Personal if publishing as yourself. Choose Organization if the publisher
is an actual organization. Organizations generally need a D-U-N-S number,
organization details and a website. Google says applying for a D-U-N-S number
through Dun & Bradstreet is free and can take up to 30 days. Google documents
limited alternatives for organizations unable to obtain one.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634885
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312

Provide your real legal name/address and reachable email/phone details. Finish
the identity and one-time-password checks shown in Console. Personal accounts
publicly display the legal name, country and developer email. Monetization and
regional requirements can make additional details public. Organization accounts
display their legal name/address and developer email/phone. Check what the
actual registration screen says will be public before completing it.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312

New personal accounts must also verify access to a non-rooted physical Android
device running Android 10 or later. As account owner, open the device task on
the Play Console Home page, scan its QR code, sign into the Play Console mobile
app with the same account, and follow Verify. An emulator cannot satisfy this.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14316361

2. Create Parimere's app entry

In Play Console, choose Home > Create app. Enter Parimere, select the default
language, choose App, set it to Free, and enter the support email. Complete the
policy/export declarations and Play App Signing terms shown there. Follow the
app Dashboard for the remaining setup tasks.

Choose the permanent package/application ID before the first bundle upload.
Package names are unique and cannot later be deleted and reused. A temporary
development ID must not accidentally become the public package identity.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152

3. Prepare the Android release

Install the Android Studio/JDK/SDK versions required by the project's pinned
Capacitor version. Prepare and sync the native web assets using the repository's
Android instructions, then open the android project in Android Studio. Build a
debug version for device checks first. A missing local toolchain leaves native
compilation and device behavior unverified even if web tests pass.

For the Play release, generate a signed Android App Bundle, ending in .aab.
New Google Play apps must use this format. Use Play App Signing and retain a
protected backup of the upload key and its recovery information outside Git.
Give each subsequent upload a larger versionCode. Keep release builds separate
from debug builds and confirm the app name, package ID and version in the final
artifact.
https://developer.android.com/guide/app-bundle
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152

Current phone-app submissions must target Android 16, API level 36, or higher.
This requirement took effect on August 31, 2026. A lower minimum supported
Android version is a separate setting and does not replace targetSdkVersion.
Check the merged release manifest and the bundle, not only a source setting.
https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk

The official 16 KB page-size guidance currently says apps targeting API 35+
must support 16 KB pages on 64-bit devices, and that updates without support
cannot be released starting February 1, 2027. Check Console's current messages
before release because this schedule can change. Apps using only Java/Kotlin
and such libraries already support 16 KB, but inspect the final artifact for
native .so libraries introduced by dependencies and test a 16 KB environment.
JavaScript/Wasm OCR assets alone are not evidence of an Android native .so
dependency. Inspect the built APK/AAB to establish what is actually packaged.
https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes

Check the merged manifest for permissions from every dependency. Occasional
receipt/label selection should use a system picker. Broad READ_MEDIA_IMAGES or
READ_MEDIA_VIDEO access is restricted to cases where a picker cannot provide
core functionality. Test cancellation, denied access and unavailable handlers.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16935362

4. Finish privacy and app-content declarations

Publish a final privacy-policy webpage on an active, public, non-geofenced URL
and link it in Console and inside the app. Google requires it even if the app
does not collect personal data. It must name Parimere or the listed developer,
give a privacy contact, explain data access/use/recipients and security, and
state retention/deletion practices. Use an ordinary non-editable webpage,
not a PDF or a document that visitors can edit.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311

Complete Data safety against the actual Android release. Include behavior from
the WebView and all plugins/SDKs. Purely local processing is excluded from
collection, while off-device transfers require assessment. User-initiated file
sharing has specific exceptions; review the definitions before answering.
Audit startup, offline work, photo reading, imports, exports and sharing using
the signed build. Check Android backup/restore as well. Do not copy answers
from the web version, whose automatic FX and optional household data flows
differ. Closed, open and production tracks require this form; apps exclusively
on internal testing are exempt from the Data safety section.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469

For the proposed native scope, there is no account creation. Confirm this in
the release build before answering account-deletion questions. Local erasure
must remove saved comparisons, catalog/history, preferences, cached app data
and any app-managed temporary exports it promises to remove. Files users saved
or shared outside Parimere remain under their control and should be described
that way. If online household identities or other accounts are added later,
review the account rule again. Apps offering account creation need an in-app
deletion path and a web deletion-request resource, covering associated data.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111

Complete every relevant App content task in Console:

- Ads: answer from the shipped build. The initial native scope has none.
  Optional ads would still require a Contains ads declaration if added later.
- App access: state whether reviewers need credentials or special steps.
  Describe how to reach file import, OCR and other features they should test.
- Target audience: choose the actual intended age groups. Including children
  brings Families requirements; do not choose an audience to bypass a policy.
- Content rating: complete the questionnaire for the actual functionality.
- Permissions and other declarations: finish all tasks Console shows.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455

Complete Financial features too. Google requires this declaration even when
an app has no financial features. Parimere compares entered shopping prices;
currency display alone does not establish banking, lending or financial advice.
Review the form's definitions and select the answer matching the shipped app.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271

5. Prepare the store listing

Keep claims specific to the shipped Android build. Describe comparison of
entered prices, quantities and offers. Do not promise live product prices,
guaranteed savings, automatic rates or household sync when they are absent.
Only advertise scanning and offline behavior after testing them on Android.

Prepare these assets:

- App name: maximum 30 characters. Parimere fits.
- Short description: maximum 80 characters.
- Full description: maximum 4,000 characters.
- Support email, category and the final privacy-policy URL.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152

- Store icon: 512 x 512 pixels, following Google's icon format requirements.
- Feature graphic: 1024 x 500 pixels, JPEG or 24-bit PNG without transparency.
- At least two actual screenshots meeting the supported dimensions. Google
  specifies 320-3840 pixels and a maximum side no more than twice the minimum.
  Four phone screenshots at 1080 x 1920 are a useful target and meet Google's
  additional screenshot guidance for recommendation formats.

Capture the actual Android UI. Include comparison entry/results, saved work,
and planning or capture only when those functions are present and verified.
The web manifest icon and desktop screenshots are not sufficient store assets.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151

6. Test, fix issues and apply for production

Start with internal testing in Play Console. Add your own Google account and
trusted testers, upload the signed AAB, install through the tester link, and
exercise the Play-delivered version. Internal testing has no 12-person or
14-day prerequisite, but it does not count as the required closed test.

Personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023 must then run a
closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for at least 14
days. The minimum must still be met when applying for production access.
Complete app setup before starting that closed test. Recruit people who will
use Parimere's workflows, keep feedback and record what was fixed. Apply from
the Dashboard when eligible and answer the questions about testing and release
readiness. Meeting the numbers allows an application; it does not guarantee
production approval. Check the requirements shown for your actual account.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465

Keep a test record for the exact version being uploaded:

[ ] Correct units, discounts, costs, rankings, ties and target quantities.
[ ] Clean install and upgrade without losing saved comparisons.
[ ] Offline launch and calculations, including local capture if advertised.
[ ] Save/reload, import/export/share and full local-data deletion.
[ ] Android Back, keyboard, rotation and background/restart behavior.
[ ] Small phones, large text, screen-reader labels and visible controls.
[ ] Camera/file selection, cancellation, denial and OCR/barcode failure cases.
[ ] No unexpected requests, SDK data flows or unneeded permissions.
[ ] No crashes or blocking issues in Play's pre-launch report and tester use.
[ ] Accurate screenshots, listing, privacy policy and Console declarations.

Fix reported issues, retest the affected workflows and retain the results.
Before submitting, confirm all pending account/Console tasks and review the
latest official requirements linked above. Keep the public web service and
privacy URL working throughout review. Google decides production access and
app approval; repository checks cannot establish either result.
